THE GUIDE
01 · THE GOAL

Reach the opposite edge

You play on a 10 × 10 board. Some squares are higher than others, so terrain plays a real role in Traversal.

Each side starts with six pieces. They begin on opposite edges and must reach the opponent’s edge. In solo mode, you control the light pieces against the bot; in two-player mode, each player controls a side.

To get started: learn the movement rules first. A good route can earn points and help you avoid risky fights.

02 · YOUR PIECES

Three pieces, three play styles

Movement is the budget for a normal route. Strength is used in combat and determines how many points a piece scores when it exits.

VanguardMovement 4 · Strength 2Gets +1 attack when the attack route costs exactly 4 points.
SentinelMovement 3 · Strength 3The strongest piece. If it is the first to exit, its side wins.
The trade-off: the stronger a piece is, the less distance it can cover in a normal move.

03 · MOVEMENT

Terrain changes the cost

Normal movement goes from one adjacent square to another, horizontally or vertically. You can chain steps while you have enough movement points.

A step on the same height costs 1 point. A one-level difference costs 2 points; a two-level difference costs 3 points. Add up the cost of every step.

A piece blocks the way: you cannot pass through an occupied square during a normal route. An enemy can be attacked if the game shows its square as a possible destination.

Example: climbing two height levels in one step costs 3 points for that step alone.

04 · THE EXPLORER

It has two shortcuts

The Explorer can make a special move one square diagonally. It can also jump two squares in a straight line, horizontally or vertically.

For a jump, the square between the start and destination must be empty. If a piece occupies it, the jump is blocked. These special moves ignore the usual terrain-based movement cost.

Watch out: the Explorer is fast, but has only 1 strength. Avoid sending it alone against a stronger piece.

05 · COMBAT

Look around before attacking

When an enemy square is offered as a destination, the game compares attack and defense. Terrain and nearby pieces can make all the difference.

  • If the attacker is higher than the target, it gets +1; if lower, it gets −1.
  • Each allied piece right next to the target adds its strength to the attack.
  • Each defending ally right next to the target adds +1 to defense.
  • The Vanguard gets another +1 attack if the full attack route costs exactly 4 points.

If attack equals or exceeds defense, the attack succeeds and the target is removed from the board.

Example: an elevated Sentinel (3, +1), supported by an allied Explorer (+1), attacks with 5. A defender with 4 is defeated.

06 · EXIT AND SCORE

A piece’s strength becomes points

Move your pieces off the opposite edge. When a piece exits, it leaves the game and its side scores points equal to its strength.

Explorer+11 point
Vanguard+22 points
Sentinel+33 points

An exited piece cannot move or fight again. Be careful: a Sentinel can win the game immediately, depending on when it exits.

Choose your plan: an Explorer scores less but is faster. A Sentinel is valuable and harder to get off the board.

07 · END OF THE GAME

How do you win?

The game ends as soon as one of these situations occurs:

  1. The first piece to exit is a Sentinel: its side wins immediately.
  2. Two pieces have exited in total: the side with the higher score wins. A tied score is a draw.
  3. A side has no pieces left on the board: each side adds the strength of its strongest remaining piece to its score, then scores are compared.
  4. The game reaches 200 moves: scores are compared. If tied, the game checks how far pieces have advanced toward the exit, then their strength.
Important detail: the Sentinel’s instant win only applies if it is truly the very first piece to exit.

08 · YOUR TURN

How to play, step by step

  1. Click one of your pieces. Possible destinations are highlighted.
  2. Click a highlighted destination. The move, attack, or exit happens immediately.
  3. Check terrain height and nearby pieces. An expensive route can still be useful, but always check combat outcomes.
  4. Rotate the camera by dragging your mouse or finger across the board.

If you have no legal move, the game automatically skips your turn.

The player icon switches between bot and two-player mode. The new-board button creates a fresh map with different terrain.

First-game tip: try moving an Explorer, check the cost shown on the squares, then gradually advance your other pieces.
